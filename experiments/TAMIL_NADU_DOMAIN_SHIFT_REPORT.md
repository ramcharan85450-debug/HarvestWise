# 1. Objective

Experiment 1 found that Tamil Nadu performed poorly under every feature configuration. This experiment asks *why*, and specifically whether the current data can distinguish between the candidate explanations: insufficient Tamil Nadu data, feature distribution shift, yield distribution shift, season-definition mismatch, and temporal domain shift.

The objective is diagnosis, not score improvement. No configuration below was tuned to make Tamil Nadu look better.

---

# 3. Geographic and temporal limitations

**This is the most important section of the report, and it constrains every result that follows.**

Region, time period, and season label are **perfectly confounded** in this dataset. Measured, not assumed:

- Years shared between AP/Telangana and Tamil Nadu: **none** (source 1999–2012, target 2019 & 2024).
- Season labels shared: **none** (source Kharif/Rabi, target Whole Year).

Isolating geography would require at least one year observed in both region groups, permitting a same-year cross-region comparison. There are zero such years. Isolating time would require the same region observed in both eras — Tamil Nadu in 1999–2012, or AP/Telangana in 2019–2024. There are none of those either.

Therefore, throughout this report, the AP+Telangana → Tamil Nadu transfer is labelled:

> **CROSS-REGION + TEMPORAL DOMAIN SHIFT** (with a season-definition change bundled in as well)

It is **not** a measurement of geographic generalization, and this report does not claim it is. A clean leakage audit certifies correct information flow inside the pipeline; it does not and cannot license a causal claim about which factor drives the failure.

# 4. State-level yield comparison

Tamil Nadu yields are shifted **upward** relative to the source domain:

| Comparison | Value |
|---|---|
| Standardized mean difference (TN vs AP+TG) | **+1.362** |
| Kolmogorov–Smirnov statistic | 0.567 |
| Wasserstein distance | 0.856 t/ha |
| Variance ratio (TN / source) | 0.831 |

The **range** overlaps almost entirely — Tamil Nadu's values sit inside the source's span, apart from a trivial 0.014 t/ha at the top. The **location** does not: Tamil Nadu's mean is 0.86 t/ha higher, and its 5th percentile (2.731) sits close to the source's median. This is a shifted distribution of similar width, not a distribution of different values. That distinction matters for §11: the target values are not exotic, they are simply systematically higher.

# 5. Weather distribution comparison

Source (AP+TG) vs Tamil Nadu, from `2B_feature_shift`:

| Feature | Source mean ± sd | Tamil Nadu mean ± sd | SMD | KS |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `weather_humidity_pct_mean` | 64.176 ± 10.887 | 75.639 ± 3.423 | **+1.42** | **0.732** |
| `weather_precip_mm_sum` | 498.662 ± 452.352 | 904.551 ± 160.967 | **+1.20** | **0.610** |
| `weather_temp_c_mean` | 26.236 ± 1.011 | 26.332 ± 1.775 | +0.07 | 0.300 |
| `weather_wind_speed_ms_mean` | 1.987 ± 0.619 | 2.120 ± 0.551 | +0.23 | 0.237 |

Humidity and precipitation shift substantially; temperature and wind speed barely move. The precipitation and humidity shift is expected in part from the season-window change alone — a Whole Year window (Jul–Jun) integrates a different and longer slice of the monsoon than a Kharif or Rabi window — so this is *not* independent evidence of a climatic difference between the states.

**A second pattern in this table matters as much as the shift in means: Tamil Nadu's feature *variance* is drastically compressed.** Humidity has a standard deviation of 3.4 in Tamil Nadu versus 10.9 in the source (a 3.2× contraction); precipitation 161 versus 452 (2.8×). This is the direct fingerprint of having only 2 years: the source's 13–14 years span wet and dry monsoons, while Tamil Nadu's rows capture two particular years. It is the quantitative form of the argument in §15 — the axis of variation the model learned from in the source domain barely exists in the target.

# 6. Satellite distribution comparison

| Feature | Source mean ± sd | Tamil Nadu mean ± sd | SMD | KS |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `satellite_ndwi_mean` | 0.068 ± 0.106 | 0.195 ± 0.054 | **+1.50** | 0.609 |
| `satellite_ndvi_mean` | 0.422 ± 0.101 | 0.555 ± 0.078 | **+1.47** | 0.605 |
| `satellite_evi_mean` | 0.285 ± 0.073 | 0.372 ± 0.047 | **+1.42** | 0.554 |

All three vegetation/water indices shift upward by roughly 1.4–1.5 pooled standard deviations. Two mechanisms are confounded here and cannot be separated: (a) Tamil Nadu genuinely being greener/wetter over its aggregation window, and (b) the 1999–2012 window being dominated by Landsat 5/7 while 2019/2024 is Landsat 8/9, with different sensors and a different scene-availability regime. Experiment 1's own alignment metadata already recorded that every Tamil Nadu row has `satellite_date_range_coverage` = 0.504 versus 1.0 for Andhra Pradesh — the Tamil Nadu windows are only half covered. **A sensor-and-coverage artefact is a live alternative explanation for this shift and is not ruled out.**

|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `weather_humidity_pct_mean` | +1.42 | 0.732 | 1.05 |
| 2 | `weather_precip_mm_sum` | +1.20 | 0.610 | 0.95 |
| 3 | `satellite_ndwi_mean` | +1.50 | 0.609 | 1.20 |

Five of seven predictive features shift by more than one pooled standard deviation. Three methods (SMD, KS, Wasserstein) agree on the ranking's top group, so the finding is not an artefact of one metric's sensitivity.

**A caution against over-reading:** KS p-values are ~0 for the top features, but Tamil Nadu contributes only 74 rows from 2 years, and those rows are not independent — districts within a year share weather systems. The p-values are therefore not trustworthy as significance tests. The **effect sizes** (SMD, Wasserstein) are the meaningful quantities, and they are large enough that the conclusion does not rest on significance testing at all.

# 8. Cross-region + temporal domain-shift experiment

**Train: Andhra Pradesh + Telangana (487 examples, 20 districts). Test: Tamil Nadu (74 examples, 38 districts).**
Validation districts were carved from the source domain only. Scaler fit on source training rows only. 5 seeds (42–46).

| Configuration | MAE ↓ | RMSE ↓ | R² ↑ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Baseline (train mean) | 0.933 ± 0.036 | 1.061 ± 0.036 | −2.188 ± 0.217 |
| Weather only | 1.075 ± 0.048 | 1.233 ± 0.056 | −3.311 ± 0.395 |
| Satellite only | **0.927 ± 0.060** | **1.055 ± 0.062** | **−2.162 ± 0.366** |
| Weather + Satellite | 1.004 ± 0.063 | 1.149 ± 0.063 | −2.744 ± 0.423 |
| Soil only | 1.320 ± 0.174 | 1.589 ± 0.226 | −6.291 ± 2.054 |
| Full multimodal | 0.939 ± 0.166 | 1.124 ± 0.214 | −2.706 ± 1.490 |

**Nothing transfers.** Every configuration has R² far below zero. The best configuration (satellite only, MAE 0.927) is statistically indistinguishable from the baseline (0.933) — that is, from ignoring the features entirely. Weather + Satellite, the winner of Experiment 1, is *worse* than the baseline here (1.004 vs 0.933).

This must be stated precisely: R² ≈ −2.2 for the baseline does not mean the model is broken. It means the AP+Telangana mean is a poor constant for Tamil Nadu — an inevitable consequence of the +0.86 t/ha level shift documented in §4. The informative comparison is not "R² < 0" but "**does any feature set beat the baseline?**" The answer is no.

# 9. Reverse exploratory experiment

**EXPLORATORY / SMALL-SOURCE-DOMAIN ANALYSIS — diagnostic only. This is not a headline result and must not be cited as one.**

Train: Tamil Nadu (74 examples, 38 districts). Test: AP + Telangana (487 examples).

| Configuration | MAE ↓ | RMSE ↓ | R² ↑ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Baseline (train mean) | 0.926 ± 0.031 | 1.063 ± 0.030 | −1.627 ± 0.146 |
| Weather only | 2.170 ± 0.589 | 2.664 ± 0.730 | −16.7 ± 8.6 |
| Satellite only | 1.791 ± 0.748 | 2.148 ± 1.008 | −12.1 ± 12.9 |
| Weather + Satellite | 3.289 ± 0.836 | 4.156 ± 1.207 | −42.5 ± 24.0 |
| Soil only | 2.009 ± 0.582 | 2.287 ± 0.519 | −11.8 ± 5.2 |
| Full multimodal | 3.928 ± 1.148 | 4.489 ± 1.134 | −48.8 ± 20.2 |

The instability is severe and exactly as predicted: standard deviations on R² are of the same order as the means. A model fit on 74 rows from 2 years, then extrapolated to feature values it never saw, produces wild predictions. The only defensible reading is directional: **the failure is symmetric.** Transfer fails in both directions, and in both directions the trained models are worse than the corresponding constant baseline. That symmetry is genuinely informative — it argues against a story in which Tamil Nadu is merely "harder" and for one in which the two domains simply do not share a usable feature→yield mapping.

# 10. Within-Tamil Nadu evaluation

Figure: `figures/domain_shift/05_within_tamil_nadu_comparison.png`

**Method:** leave-one-district-out over all 38 Tamil Nadu districts, repeated for 5 seeds (1,140 model fits). LOGO was chosen over repeated GroupShuffleSplit because Tamil Nadu has ~1.9 examples per district: a 20% test fold holds ~15 rows, and R² on 15 rows from 2 years is too unstable to interpret. LOGO instead yields a strictly out-of-fold prediction for **every one of the 74 examples**, with the metric computed once over the pooled predictions. Validation districts are drawn from the remaining training districts, never the held-out one.

| Configuration | MAE ↓ | RMSE ↓ | R² ↑ |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Baseline (train mean)** | **0.472 ± 0.002** | **0.607 ± 0.002** | **−0.043 ± 0.006** |
| Weather only | 0.626 ± 0.040 | 0.809 ± 0.063 | −0.861 ± 0.289 |
| Satellite only | 0.521 ± 0.059 | 0.652 ± 0.098 | −0.232 ± 0.401 |
| Weather + Satellite | 0.687 ± 0.059 | 0.898 ± 0.092 | −1.307 ± 0.496 |
| Soil only | 1.000 ± 0.310 | 1.279 ± 0.365 | −4.005 ± 3.017 |
| Full multimodal | 0.935 ± 0.086 | 1.238 ± 0.125 | −3.384 ± 0.870 |

**This is the single most consequential result in Experiment 2, and it is a negative one.** Even when the model is trained on Tamil Nadu data and tested on Tamil Nadu data — no cross-region gap, no era gap, no season-definition change — **no feature configuration beats the constant baseline.** Every one is worse, several by a wide margin.

The absolute errors are much smaller than in §8 (MAE 0.47 vs 0.93), which confirms that the *level offset* is a real and large component of the cross-region failure. But the *ordering* is unchanged: features do not help.

Honest statement of instability: the baseline's variation across seeds is negligible (± 0.002, as expected for a near-deterministic quantity), but soil-only varies by ± 0.31 MAE and ± 3.0 R². Any claim resting on the exact value of a non-baseline configuration here would be unstable. The claim that *none of them beats the baseline* is not — the gap exceeds the seed variation in every case.

# 11. Tamil Nadu failure analysis

Figure: `figures/domain_shift/03_cross_region_actual_vs_predicted.png`

Primary model: Weather + Satellite trained on AP + Telangana, predictions averaged over the 5 seeds.

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| Mean residual (bias) | **+0.878 t/ha** (model systematically under-predicts) |
| Mean absolute residual | 1.001 t/ha |
| Prediction std | 0.346 | 
| Actual std | 0.599 |
| Prediction range | 2.30 – 3.99 t/ha |
| Actual range | 1.97 – 5.27 t/ha |
| **Correlation(prediction, actual)** | **−0.154** |

Two distinct failures, and they need to be separated:

1. **A level offset.** The model predicts around the source mean while Tamil Nadu sits 0.86 t/ha higher, producing a +0.878 t/ha bias.
2. **A collapse of ranking ability.** Correlation between prediction and truth is **−0.154** — slightly negative. The model does not merely predict too low; it does not order Tamil Nadu districts correctly at all. The prediction range (1.7 t/ha wide) is also compressed against the actual range (3.3 t/ha wide), the classic regression-to-the-mean signature.

**Error decomposition** (`2H_failure_breakdown.error_decomposition`):

| | MAE | RMSE | R² |
|---|---|---|---|
| As predicted | 1.001 | 1.143 | −2.694 |
| Oracle-debiased (true mean residual added back) | 0.563 | 0.731 | −0.513 |
| Tamil Nadu's own mean | 0.462 | 0.594 | 0.000 |

The constant offset accounts for **59% of the mean squared error**. But granting the model oracle knowledge of the true offset still leaves R² = **−0.513** — still worse than predicting Tamil Nadu's own mean. (The oracle row uses information the model never had; it is a diagnostic upper bound on what recalibration could buy, not a reportable score.)

**Conclusion: intercept recalibration would fix most of the error's magnitude and still not produce a useful model.** This rules out the most optimistic hypothesis — that Tamil Nadu just needs a bias correction.

# 12. Residual analysis

Figure: `figures/domain_shift/04_tamil_nadu_residual_distribution.png`

**By year:**

| Year | n | Mean residual | Std |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2019 | 36 | +1.137 | 0.717 |
| 2024 | 38 | +0.633 | 0.675 |

Both years are under-predicted; 2019 roughly twice as badly as 2024. With only two years, this difference cannot be attributed to anything — it is reported as an observation, not explained.

**By season:** all 74 Tamil Nadu examples are `Whole Year`. No within-Tamil-Nadu season comparison is possible.

**By yield level:** correlation between residual and actual yield is **+0.885**. This is the dominant pattern in the residuals, and it is a mechanical consequence of the compressed prediction range rather than an independent finding: when predictions barely vary, the residual is approximately (actual − constant), which correlates with actual almost perfectly. It says the model fails hardest on high-yielding districts *because* it fails to differentiate districts at all.

**By district:** per-district mean residuals are in the JSON (`2H_failure_breakdown.per_district`). With 1–2 observations per district, per-district residuals are not separable from noise, and no district-specific claim is made here.

Patterns explicitly **not** claimed: no relationship to any particular district was identified, and no causal mechanism for the 2019/2024 difference is proposed. Only the patterns above are visible in the data.

# 13. Evidence for possible causes

| Hypothesis | Verdict | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Insufficient Tamil Nadu training examples** | **Supported, and it is a structural problem, not just a size problem** | §10: with in-domain LOGO training, nothing beats the mean. But note *what* is missing: 74 rows across only 2 years means there is almost no temporal yield variation for a weather-driven model to learn from — the variance is nearly all cross-district. More rows from the same 2 years would likely not fix this. |
| **B. Feature distribution shift** | **Confirmed, large** | §7: 5 of 7 features shift by >1 pooled sd; SMD, KS and Wasserstein agree. Partly attributable to the season-window change and possibly to the Landsat-sensor change, so its *cause* is not isolated. |
| **C. Yield distribution shift** | **Confirmed, large** | §4: SMD +1.36, mean offset +0.86 t/ha, KS 0.567. Accounts for 59% of the cross-region MSE (§11). |
| **D. Season-definition mismatch** | **INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE** | Perfectly confounded with region. Kharif/Rabi vs Whole Year cannot be separated from AP/TG vs TN with these rows. Mechanistically plausible — a Whole Year window aggregates a different slice of the monsoon — but untestable here. |
| **E. Temporal domain shift** | **INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE** | Perfectly confounded with region: zero overlapping years. §14 quantifies what *can* be said. |
| **F. A combination** | **This is the supported conclusion** | B and C are measured and large; A is demonstrated independently by §10; D and E are unmeasurable but not excluded. |

**Partial evidence on E** (`2G_temporal_confound`): within Andhra Pradesh and Telangana, comparing their own early half (1999–2005) to their own late half (2006–2012), feature shifts are small — SMDs of 0.01 to 0.28, and 0.43 for yield. The AP/TG → Tamil Nadu shift is a **median of 5.3× larger** across features. This is consistent with the cross-region gap being more than ordinary decade-scale drift. It is **not** proof: 2019/2024 lies entirely outside the observed source period, so drift in that era cannot be measured, and a non-linear acceleration after 2012 (plausible for both climate and Landsat sensor generation) would not be visible in this comparison.

**Overall answer to 2I: F — a combination of factors, with B and C measured directly, A demonstrated independently, and D and E constituting INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE.**

# 14. What cannot be concluded

Stated plainly, because these are the claims a reader would most plausibly over-extend:

1. **Not** that the model fails on Tamil Nadu *because Tamil Nadu is geographically different.* Geography, era, and season definition are perfectly collinear here.
2. **Not** that this experiment measures pure cross-region or geographic generalization. It does not, and no reanalysis of these rows can make it do so.
3. **Not** that the observed satellite shift is a climatic or agronomic fact. A Landsat 5/7 → 8/9 sensor change and a 0.504 vs 1.0 window-coverage difference are unexcluded alternative explanations.
4. **Not** that "Southern India generalization" has been demonstrated. It has been *refuted* for the one out-of-source region tested.
5. **Not** any causal claim from §9 (reverse transfer), whose seed-to-seed variation is as large as its effects.
6. **Not** that more Tamil Nadu rows alone would fix the problem — §10 shows features fail in-domain too, and the missing dimension appears to be *years*, not rows.
7. **Not** that Experiment 1's Weather + Satellite result is invalidated. It stands for the districts and period it was measured on; it simply does not extend to this target domain.

# 15. Scientific interpretation

Experiment 1 established that Weather + Satellite generalizes modestly to unseen districts (R² 0.205) when those districts are drawn from the same states, years and seasons as the training data. Experiment 2 establishes the boundary of that result: it does not survive a simultaneous change of region, era and season definition, and it does not reappear when the model is retrained inside the target domain.

The most useful finding is the one from §10 and §11 together. The cross-region failure is *not* primarily a calibration problem — an oracle intercept correction leaves R² at −0.51 — and it is *not* solely a domain-gap problem either, because the same features fail in a clean within-Tamil Nadu evaluation. What both point to is that in Tamil Nadu's 2-year, 38-district slice, the 12 environmental features carry essentially no district-discriminating yield signal. The dominant axis of variation available to the model in the source domain was year-to-year climate variation across 13–14 years; Tamil Nadu offers 2 years, so that axis is almost absent, and what remains — between-district variation within a year — is exactly the axis Experiment 1 already showed the soil features fail on.

This is a coherent negative result rather than a bug, and it is more informative than the headline number from Experiment 1: the framework learns *temporal* yield response, not *spatial* yield level, and a target domain that provides only spatial variation therefore defeats it.

# 16. Conference-paper implications

**What can be claimed honestly:**

- An unseen-district evaluation protocol with a 9-check pre-training leakage audit, applied to 561 real, officially-sourced district-season examples across three Indian states.
- A modest but real positive result (Experiment 1): Weather + Satellite beats a train-mean baseline on unseen districts within the source domain.
- A well-characterized negative result (Experiment 2): that model does not transfer to a region separated in space, time and season definition, and the failure is decomposed into a level shift (59% of MSE) and a collapse of ranking ability that recalibration cannot repair.
- An explicit demonstration that a clean leakage audit does not license a causal claim — the confounding in §3 is a design limitation no code discipline can remove.

**What must not be claimed:**

- "Southern India generalization." Refuted for Tamil Nadu.
- "Cross-region / geographic generalization" as the framing of §8. It is cross-region **and** temporal **and** season-definition shift.
- Any multimodal fusion benefit. Full multimodal is worse than Weather + Satellite in Experiment 1 and worse than the baseline throughout Experiment 2.
- Any claim that soil features contribute. Soil-only is the worst configuration in every single evaluation in both experiments.

**Recommended framing:** present Experiment 2 as a domain-shift limitations study accompanying Experiment 1, not as a second headline result. Reviewers are more likely to credit a paper that identifies the boundary of its own positive result than one that reports only the positive result.

# Final questions

**1. Does Tamil Nadu represent a measurable domain shift? — YES.**
Large and consistent across three independent measures. Five of seven features shift by >1 pooled standard deviation (KS up to 0.732); yield shifts by SMD +1.36 with a +0.86 t/ha mean offset.

**2. Is the shift mainly geographic, temporal, or both? — CANNOT BE SEPARATED; it is at minimum both, plus a season-definition change.**
Zero overlapping years and zero overlapping season labels between the domains make the three factors perfectly collinear. Partial evidence (§13) shows the cross-domain shift is ~5.3× larger than within-source decade-scale drift, which suggests it is not *purely* era drift — but that falls well short of isolating geography.

**3. Does Weather + Satellite generalize from AP/Telangana to Tamil Nadu? — NO.**
MAE 1.004 vs the baseline's 0.933; R² −2.744. It is *worse than ignoring the features*. Correlation between its predictions and the truth is −0.154.

**4. Does soil generalize across regions? — NO, decisively, and it is the worst configuration everywhere.**
Cross-region MAE 1.320 (worst of six); within-Tamil Nadu MAE 1.000 vs a 0.472 baseline. This is consistent with, and extends, Experiment 1's finding that soil acts as a location fingerprint rather than an agronomic signal — a fingerprint is worthless in a region whose locations were never seen.

**5. Can the current data isolate the cause of Tamil Nadu failure? — PARTIALLY.**
It *can* establish: feature shift is large (measured), yield shift is large (measured), the offset explains 59% of MSE but correcting it is insufficient (measured), and the features fail even in-domain within Tamil Nadu (measured). It *cannot* separate geographic from temporal from season-definition effects — those are perfectly confounded, and the honest verdict there is **INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE**.

**6. Is more Tamil Nadu data necessary? — YES, but the *kind* matters more than the quantity.**
§10 shows that simply adding more rows from 2019 and 2024 would probably not help: with only two years, there is almost no temporal yield variation to learn from, and the features already fail on the cross-district axis. What is needed is more **years**, not more rows.

**7. What is the single highest-priority next data collection task? — Collect Tamil Nadu district yields plus aligned weather and satellite for a contiguous run of years overlapping the AP/Telangana period (1999–2012), or extend AP/Telangana to 2019–2024.**

Either one breaks the confound that makes questions 2 and 5 unanswerable, by creating years observed in both region groups. That single change converts the central untestable question into a testable one and would let a same-year cross-region comparison isolate geography. It also directly addresses the finding in question 6, since it supplies the temporal variation Tamil Nadu currently lacks.

Two secondary tasks, in order: (a) fix the Tamil Nadu satellite window coverage, currently 0.504 versus 1.0 for Andhra Pradesh, by fetching the missing Jan–Jun half of each Whole Year window — this removes one of the two alternative explanations for the satellite shift in §6; (b) close the 1997–1999 and 2013–2014 environmental gaps for AP/Telangana, which would raise usable examples from 561 toward 868.
